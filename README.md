Equipment rental is a common business in Sri Lanka for events, photography, construction and education. Small rental shops still manage cameras, projectors, sound systems, tools and event equipment with phone calls, WhatsApp messages, paper diaries and cash receipts. This leads to double bookings, lost deposits, no proof of handover, and very little visibility for customers about what is actually available.
RentEase is a web-based Equipment Rental Management System developed by Group 12 for the ICT2232 Software Engineering mini project. It brings customers, rental shops (equipment providers), delivery partners and a platform administrator into one system. Customers can search equipment, check date availability, book single items or packages, choose pickup or delivery, pay and track their rental. Shops manage listings and approve requests. Delivery partners accept dispatch tasks, and administrators control verification, users, pricing settings, coupons and reports.
This report documents how the project progressed through every stage of the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) — from problem definition to requirements, design, implementation, testing, deployment, maintenance and security — and how artefacts from one stage informed the next.



RentEase successfully addresses the problems of manual equipment rental: double bookings are prevented by transactional availability checks, renters are verified, every handover is proven by one-time codes, and prices, deposits and delivery charges are calculated consistently. Following Agile Scrum let the team deliver working increments every sprint and absorb new requirements such as identity verification and delivery dispatch.
The project demonstrates a complete SDLC: requirements traced to design, code and tests; a documented architecture and database; automated and manual testing with a defect log; cloud deployment on Cloudflare, R2 and MongoDB Atlas; a realistic adaptive maintenance change; and a security assessment with implemented controls. Future work includes a live payment gateway, Sinhala/Tamil localisation, a mobile app and analytics for demand forecasting.


Beyond delivering a working system, the project gave the team practical experience of applying software engineering principles rather than only writing code. Using Scrum showed how a prioritised backlog and short iterations make it possible to respond to change, as when identity verification and delivery dispatch were introduced mid-project. Writing requirements first and tracing them to tests made it easier to find and fix defects early, and applying defence in depth showed that security depends on several independent layers rather than a single control. Each team member contributed to requirements, design, implementation and testing, and version control provided evidence of this collaboration. The main limitations are the absence of a live payment gateway and the lack of native mobile and multilingual support, which are identified as future work.
